Training: Preparing for Competition

What Training Looks Like

Training for women's wrestling involves a combination of physical conditioning, technical skill development, and mental preparation. Athletes typically engage in strength training, cardiovascular exercise, and technique drills to improve their performance.

Strength vs. Conditioning vs. Technique

Strength training focuses on building muscle power and endurance, which are essential for executing moves and maintaining performance throughout a match. Wrestlers lift weights at least twice a week to build strength. Conditioning exercises help improve cardiovascular fitness and overall stamina, allowing athletes to sustain their energy levels during competition. On average, most will probably run more than 6 miles a week in total for conditioning. Technique training or wrestling practices emphasize the proper execution of wrestling moves and strategies, ensuring that athletes can effectively apply their skills in real-world scenarios. Practices range from 1.5 to 2 or more hours and the number of practices varies by training phase like preseason, in-season, and post season.

How Practices Typically Go

  1. Stretch before practice
  2. Warm up with running and drills or if you're lucky maybe a game of some kind like handball
  3. Partner stretches
  4. Drills and technique work
  5. Live wrestling
  6. A little bit of conditioning
  7. Cool down and stretch

Nutrition and Hydration

Proper nutrition and hydration are essential for optimal performance in women's wrestling. Athletes should consume a balanced diet that includes a variety of nutrients to support energy levels, muscle recovery, and overall health. Staying hydrated before, during, and after training sessions is also crucial to prevent dehydration and maintain peak performance.

Recovery and Rest

Recovery and rest are crucial components of a wrestler's training regimen. Adequate rest allows the body to repair and strengthen muscles, while proper recovery techniques help prevent injuries and reduce fatigue. Athletes often incorporate activities like stretching, foam rolling, and massage into their recovery routine.
